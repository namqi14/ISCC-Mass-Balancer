For crosschecking of the Mass Balance tools:
1. Scheme-Specific and Scope-Specific Ledgers
      * Separate by Scheme and Scope: PEGT must maintain separate mass balance accounting and calculations for each certified scope (e.g., Trader with Storage, Processing Unit). The ledgers must keep mass balances separate for different materials, sites, and certification scopes, meaning ISCC EU and ISCC PLUS transactions cannot be mixed in the same ledger.
      * Clear Documentation: The mass balance must transparently document monthly inputs, outputs, and physical stock. Sustainable and non-sustainable materials must be clearly separated within the bookkeeping.
2. Strict Site-Specific Segregation
      * Geographical Boundaries: Both ISCC EU and ISCC PLUS mandate that mass balance bookkeeping and calculations must be strictly site-specific.
      * Individual Locations: PEGT must keep a separate quantity bookkeeping (e.g., mass balance ledger) for every individual storage location or processing site used. It is not permitted to aggregate mass balances across different geographical locations.
      * Multiple Legal Entities: If more than one legal entity operates at a single location, each legal entity is required to operate its own separate mass balance bookkeeping.


3. Material Segregation Rules
      * ISCC EU (Annex IX Materials): Different raw materials must be kept separately in the mass balance. Materials can only be grouped into a single "product group" if they share similar physical and chemical characteristics, possess similar heating values, and fall under the exact same RED III category (e.g., Annex IX Part A or Annex IX Part B).
      * ISCC PLUS (Bio-Circular Materials): The mass balance must be kept material-specific, indicating the respective raw material. Furthermore, different raw material categories (e.g., bio, bio-circular, circular, renewable) must be kept completely separate within the mass balance calculation.
4. Characteristics and Claims Assignment
      * Unique Assignment: The bookkeeping must allow sets of sustainability characteristics to be uniquely identified and assigned to individual incoming and outgoing batches. These sets of characteristics must not be split.
      * ISCC EU Data: For Annex IX materials, the ledger must retain information such as the country of origin, the RED III waste/residue definition, and GHG emissions values. Different GHG values cannot be aggregated. However, the highest (least performing) GHG emission value among incoming batches with identical characteristics can be applied consistently to all of those batches.
      * ISCC PLUS Data: For bio-circular materials, the ledger and corresponding Sustainability Declarations must include the raw material category, the waste status (e.g., pre-consumer, post-consumer, or mixed), and the claim "ISCC Compliant".
5. Managing Credits and Deficits
      * Zero Deficits: A negative balance of credits is strictly prohibited under both ISCC EU and ISCC PLUS. At no point can PEGT withdraw more sustainable material than what was added, including the existing inventory at the start of the period.
      * ISCC EU Carry-Overs: Positive credits for ISCC EU materials can only be transferred to the next mass balance period if an equivalent amount of physical material is physically available in stock.
      * ISCC PLUS Credit Transfers: Processing units and storage facilities certified under ISCC PLUS are permitted to transfer credits between different sites. This is only allowed if the supplier and recipient of the credits are part of the same company or corporate group, the sites are located within national borders or neighboring countries, and the transfer applies to the same kind of outgoing product.

To ensure your digital platform complies with all ISCC mass balance and quantity bookkeeping rules, it must be architected to handle strict geographical boundaries, prevent deficits, and manage the distinct credit carryover rules between ISCC EU and ISCC PLUS.


Here is the comprehensive compliance checklist for your system's development:


1. System Architecture & Ledger Segregation
Your database architecture must prevent the co-mingling of data that ISCC requires to be kept separate.
      * Site-Specific Boundaries: The system must generate and maintain separate mass balance ledgers for every individual geographical location, storage facility, or interconnected grid. Aggregating mass balances across different geographic sites is strictly prohibited.
      * Scope Segregation: The platform must separate ledgers based on the certified scope (e.g., a "Trader with Storage" ledger must be distinct from a "Processing Unit" ledger).
      * Scheme Isolation: ISCC EU and ISCC PLUS ledgers must be completely segregated; the system logic must block any transfer of credits from an ISCC PLUS balance into an ISCC EU balance.
      * Material Categorization (ISCC PLUS): For ISCC PLUS, the system must separate raw material categories (Bio, Bio-circular, Circular, Renewable-energy-derived) within the ledger.
      * Product Grouping (ISCC EU): For ISCC EU, the system must keep different raw materials separate unless they meet the strict criteria for a "product group" (sharing similar physical and chemical characteristics, similar heating values, and the exact same RED III category).
2. Data Intake & Batch Verification
The platform must act as the gatekeeper for all incoming sustainable material.
      * Physical Linkage: The system must require every virtual sustainable batch entry to be linked to a physical receipt document (e.g., weighbridge ticket, pipeline nomination, or bill of lading).
      * Sustainability Characteristics: The system must capture all mandatory fields from incoming Sustainability Declarations (SDs), including raw material type, country of origin, and Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emission values.
      * No Averaging of Characteristics: When batches with different sustainability characteristics are physically mixed, the database must maintain them as distinct rows or subsets; the system cannot average differing GHG values.
      * Worst-Case GHG Option: If multiple incoming batches have different GHG values but are otherwise identical, the system logic may allow users to apply the highest (worst-performing) GHG value across all those specific batches to simplify bookkeeping.




3. Processing & Conversion Engine
If the platform is used by a Processing Unit, it must accurately calculate how much output can be claimed as certified.
      * Operational Conversion Factors: The system must require the input of site-specific conversion factors (CF) based on actual operational data to account for yields and process losses.
      * Output Calculation: The system must calculate the maximum allowable certified output by multiplying the incoming certified mass by this conversion factor ().
      * Methodology Lock: The system should prevent users from changing their chosen conversion or consumption factor methodology in the middle of a certification period.
4. Ledger Balancing & Deficit Rules
This is the core math engine of the mass balance system.
         * Period Enforcement: The system must enforce defined mass balance periods capped at a maximum of three (3) months.
         * Continuous Accounting: Periods must be continuous without gaps; the system must automatically open a new period even if no material movement occurs.
         * The Golden Rule: The system logic must enforce that the total outgoing certified material never exceeds the sum of the opening certified stock plus incoming certified material for that period.
         * Hard Deficit Block: The system must throw a hard error and prevent ledger closure if the calculation results in a negative balance (deficit).
         * Gas Grid Exception: For biomethane or gas injected into a transmission grid, the system must strictly monitor the balance in real-time to ensure it never goes into deficit at any point during the period, not just at the end.
5. Credit Carryover & Scheme Differences
Your system must deploy different logical rules for surplus credits based on the certification scheme.
         * Credit Calculation: At the end of a period, the system calculates surplus credits ().
         * ISCC EU Carryover Check: To carry positive credits into the next period under ISCC EU, the system must verify against physical inventory data. Credits can only be transferred if an equivalent amount of physical material (sustainable or non-sustainable) is currently held in stock at that specific site.
         * ISCC PLUS Carryover Check: Under ISCC PLUS, the system must allow positive credits to carry forward with no time limit, regardless of whether any physical material is currently in stock.
         * Multi-Site Transfers (ISCC PLUS Only): The system may allow credit transfers between different sites under ISCC PLUS only if it verifies the sites belong to the same company or corporate group, are within national borders or neighboring countries, and are handling the same kind of product.
6. Audit Readiness & Reporting
The platform must generate the evidence required by third-party auditors.
            * Periodic Reporting: The system must automatically generate periodic reports (e.g., monthly) detailing opening/closing stocks and incoming/outgoing quantities for both certified and non-certified materials.
            * Data Retention: The database must securely retain all mass balance ledgers, linked contracts, delivery notes, and SDs for a minimum of five (5) years.
